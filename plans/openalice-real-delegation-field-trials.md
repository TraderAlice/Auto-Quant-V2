## Outcome

Use Grok 4.5 as a deliberately capable but non-frontier coding coworker to
receive realistic “quantitative research support needed” assignments, operate
an installed AutoQuant desk without framework-developer coaching, and expose
which current abstractions genuinely help or hinder the work.

Turn repeated or severe observed friction into the smallest general AutoQuant
improvement on the `0.9.0` line, then prove each material change with a
different fresh Grok 4.5 worker. Preserve positive, negative, unsupported, and
failed research outcomes equally when they are scientifically honest.

## Governing model

OpenAlice delegates a task to a quantitative coworker at another persistent
desk. AutoQuant remains independently usable; the outward handoff may be
ordinary prose plus exact evidence references. No transport protocol, rigid
natural-language router, automatic Git upgrader, or OpenAlice-specific Core
surface is introduced unless repeated real work demonstrates that it is
necessary.

Harness checkout upgrades remain ordinary Coding Agent work. A worker may
pull and resolve Git conflicts, or retire an old Harness and create a new one.
This plan does not add `aq upgrade`, automatic migration, or a compatibility
matrix.

## Worker isolation

Every qualifying first-pass worker receives only:

- one fresh clone of the exact released AutoQuant tag used by OpenAlice, with
  its original `.git` history and remote preserved, plus that release's
  installed environment;
- the clone's repository-root Workspace and checked-in teaching Project;
- the ordinary caller-style assignment and any caller-supplied facts;
- public network access and the Skills materialized by that release;
- normal shell and filesystem tools inside its desk.

It does not receive:

- any newer AutoQuant checkout, unreleased fixes, external previous
  field-trial directories, or hidden answer Project;
- private framework explanations, prepared commands, hidden answer keys, or
  corrective live coaching;
- prior Grok memory, web-search summaries, subagents, or another model's
  research;
- permission to access accounts, place Orders, or claim trading authority.

A worker may inspect the checked-out Harness source and documentation because
that is a deliberate benefit of the real OpenAlice clone model. The
observation record notes whether ordinary orientation was sufficient or the
worker had to reverse-engineer Core.

A worker may ask a material clarification question. The delegating reviewer
answers only the caller-owned ambiguity and records the exchange; it does not
teach framework mechanics.

## Cohort

The cohort uses materially different decision questions rather than repeated
factor demos:

1. **Mainland-China cross-section to target weights.** Acquire at least two
   peer routes for named XSHG/XSHE/XBSE equities, test a bounded
   price/volume hypothesis, and either produce qualified long-only target
   weights or explain why weights are not supported.
2. **Price-event behavior.** Answer a post-event path question with one fixed
   event definition and preserve a useful negative result.
3. **Reported-book support.** Audit one caller-reported holding snapshot and
   answer a bounded crowding, risk-budget, or funded-change question without
   inventing live account truth.
4. **Taiwan venue research.** Use official TWSE first and at least one peer
   route, preserving authority, adjustment, missing-row, and freshness
   semantics.
5. **Multi-interval or continuous-market context.** Use a base interval plus
   causal completed higher-interval features and prove that the Agent
   understands the decision clock.
6. **Governed RL decision.** Determine whether adaptive policy evidence adds
   value beyond a fixed mechanical benchmark inside the declared time budget;
   retaining the baseline is a valid result.

At least one final case must end with no stable rule, no justified target
weights, or an explicit unsupported boundary. The cohort is not accepted if
every assignment is made artificially positive.

## Observation record

For every worker preserve:

- exact release/wheel hash, model id, prompt, Workspace, and start/end time;
- clarification exchanges and caller-owned facts;
- command transcript, retries, elapsed time, and network/provider failures;
- selected Project/Study route and whether selection was independently
  justified;
- data sources, raw/package/snapshot hashes, semantic comparison, and
  selection reason;
- edits, Checks, Experiments, Runs, Reports/Dossiers, final orientation, and
  Studio snapshot where applicable;
- outward answer and exact evidence references;
- scientific, authority, recoverability, and user-usefulness review;
- each friction's owning layer and disposition.

Worker quality is separate from market outcome. A clarification, REVERT,
negative result, or refusal may pass. Hidden intent changes, test leakage,
unsupported weights, provider-semantic relabelling, or trading claims fail.

## Friction policy

- Fix a scientific-integrity, evidence-identity, destructive-state, or
  authority defect immediately.
- Fix lower-severity framework friction only after it recurs in two
  independent tasks, unless it prevents a complete first task.
- Keep provider-specific access failures in the provider Skill or ledger.
- Keep task-specific research choices in the Project.
- Do not add Core convenience merely because the framework-developer knows a
  shorter private route.
- Retry every material framework change with a fresh Grok 4.5 worker that has
  not seen the original failure.
